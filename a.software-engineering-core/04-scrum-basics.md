# Scrum Basics

## Overview

Scrum is a framework used within Agile to help teams organize and manage software projects.

It divides work into small development cycles called **sprints**, allowing teams to build, test, and improve software continuously.

Scrum emphasizes teamwork, communication, and regular feedback.

Today, Scrum is one of the most widely used frameworks in software engineering.

---

## Why Scrum Matters

Scrum helps teams:

- Organize their work
- Deliver software faster
- Improve collaboration
- Adapt to changes
- Track progress
- Reduce risks

Scrum makes large projects easier to manage.

---

## How Scrum Works

A typical Scrum workflow looks like this:

```text
Product Backlog

↓

Sprint Planning

↓

Sprint

↓

Daily Stand-up

↓

Sprint Review

↓

Sprint Retrospective

↓

Next Sprint
```

The cycle repeats until the project is completed.

---

## Scrum Roles

Scrum teams usually consist of three main roles.

### Product Owner

The Product Owner represents the customer and decides what features should be built.

Responsibilities:

- Define requirements
- Prioritize tasks
- Manage the product backlog
- Communicate with stakeholders

---

### Scrum Master

The Scrum Master ensures that the team follows Scrum principles.

Responsibilities:

- Remove obstacles
- Organize meetings
- Help the team stay productive
- Improve workflows

---

### Development Team

The development team builds the product.

Responsibilities:

- Write code
- Test software
- Fix bugs
- Deliver features

---

## Scrum Artifacts

Scrum uses three important artifacts.

### Product Backlog

A list of all features and tasks.

Example:

```text
- Login system

- User profiles

- Payment gateway

- Search feature
```

---

### Sprint Backlog

The tasks selected for the current sprint.

Example:

```text
Sprint 1

- Login page

- Registration page
```

---

### Increment

The working software produced at the end of a sprint.

Example:

```text
A fully functional login system
```

---

## Scrum Events

Scrum includes five important events.

### 1. Sprint Planning

The team decides what will be built during the sprint.

Questions:

- What should we build?
- How will we build it?

---

### 2. Daily Stand-up

A short daily meeting, usually 15 minutes.

Each team member answers:

- What did I do yesterday?
- What will I do today?
- What problems am I facing?

---

### 3. Sprint

The actual development period.

Typical duration:

- One week
- Two weeks
- Four weeks

---

### 4. Sprint Review

The team presents the completed work to stakeholders.

Example:

```text
"We finished the login and registration system."
```

---

### 5. Sprint Retrospective

The team discusses:

- What went well?
- What went wrong?
- How can we improve?

The goal is continuous improvement.

## Advantages of Scrum

Scrum provides:

- Better teamwork
- Faster development
- Continuous feedback
- Improved productivity
- Better organization
- Higher software quality

---

## Disadvantages of Scrum

Scrum also has some challenges:

- Requires strong communication
- Needs active participation
- Can be difficult for inexperienced teams
- Frequent meetings can consume time

Despite these challenges, Scrum remains extremely popular.

---

## Scrum vs Agile

| Agile | Scrum |
| --- | --- |
| A methodology | A framework |
| General principles | Specific rules |
| Flexible approach | Structured process |

In simple words:

```text
Agile = Philosophy

Scrum = One way to apply Agile
```

---

## Common Mistakes Beginners Make

Beginners often:

- Think Agile and Scrum are the same thing.
- Ignore daily stand-ups.
- Create very large sprints.
- Skip retrospectives.
- Confuse the Scrum Master with a project manager.

Remember:

Scrum is a framework that helps teams apply Agile principles.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Scrum is a framework used in Agile development.
- Scrum divides work into sprints.
- Scrum has three main roles.
- Scrum uses backlogs to organize work.
- Scrum includes meetings such as stand-ups and retrospectives.
- Scrum focuses on teamwork and continuous improvement.

---

## Summary

Scrum is an Agile framework that helps teams organize and manage software projects through short development cycles called sprints. By using defined roles, events, and artifacts, Scrum enables teams to collaborate effectively, adapt to changes, and deliver high-quality software incrementally.
